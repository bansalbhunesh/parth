1. The problem

In a hyperscale data-centre EPC build, the design basis, vendor submittals, and governing standards live in different systems and are reviewed by different people. A vendor deviation missed at submittal review resurfaces months later at commissioning — as a failed acceptance test, rework, and schedule slip. Found on submittal day it is a one-line RFI; found at commissioning it is a schedule event.

Market context scenario: India data-centre capex is large and growing (IBEF, secondary research: ~US$10B in 2025 toward ~US$22B by 2030; JLL primary release is more conservative — 791 MW added by 2026, US$5.7B investment). These frame the opportunity; they are not Pramaan-specific results.

2. What Pramaan does

  • Reconciles a design specification against a vendor submittal and the governing standards, and reports each genuine deviation with the standard it violates.
  • For each deviation, predicts the commissioning test that will fail and the lead-time-in-weeks to fix it if caught now — the commissioning-risk twin.
  • Renders an evidence chain (required value, submitted value, source citation, confidence) and exports an audit trail for the commissioning authority.
  • Degrades gracefully: when the LLM is unavailable, an independent rule-based detector still catches the headline numeric shortfalls — no silent zeros, and never the answer key (see §5).

3. Architecture (as actually executed)

Pramaan is one compliance/Cx reasoning graph plus three connected deterministic intelligence services — not five co-operating AI agents. The runtime compliance graph is:

ingest → load_standards → validate → reconcile → retrieve → critique → cx_predict → format_output

  • validate is a routing gate; reconcile/retrieve/critique form a bounded retrieval + reflexion loop; cx_predict maps each deviation to its commissioning test via a rule table (no per-deviation LLM call).
  • The standalone extraction module is not in the executed graph, and the RFI Copilot is a separate API workflow — stated plainly rather than diagrammed as active agents.
  • The three deterministic services — schedule risk (graph CPM + seeded beta-PERT Monte Carlo), supply chain (closed-form ETA/risk over static data), and the project/Cx graph — are separate endpoints, not agents in the compliance graph.

5. Scientific integrity of the analysis path

User-facing analysis never substitutes the answer key. When the LLM pipeline returns empty (throttled / no key), the corpus endpoints degrade to the independent rule detector over the same spec + submittal, labelled analysis_mode:"rule", or report analysis_mode:"unavailable" — never the seeded ground-truth labels. The seeded fixture is reachable only via an explicit ?seeded_demo=true opt-in, stamped analysis_mode:"seeded_demo" with a disclaimer. The copilot's offline fallback is labelled mode:"offline-fallback". This is verified by automated tests that assert seeded identifiers never leak into the default path.

6. Evaluation methodology & results

6a. Synthetic portfolio — breadth check synthetic

A 12-project, 11-country synthetic corpus with seeded deviations gives repeatable breadth coverage. Its P/R/F1 read 1.000 by construction — it exercises the plumbing across formats and standards; it is not evidence of external accuracy, and we label it that way rather than headline it.

6b. Team-authored evaluation pairs — the credibility signal

Fifteen team-authored pairs whose values are cited from public product datasheets and standards (Vertiv, Cummins, STULZ, ABB, Tate ConCore, Schneider Canalis, Raritan, Xtralis VESDA, Distech, and others). They are realistic engineering documents, not downloaded immutable vendor files. Across them are 27 hard deviation claims + 1 deliberately contested case.

LayerWhat it provesResult
Deterministic offline deterministic offlineRule detector recovers headline numeric shortfalls with no API key; clears compliant/true-negative values4 of the 27 hard claims + true-negatives; 0 false positives, reproducible (python eval/real_pairs_offline.py)
Live-model live-modelFrontier model recovers the reasoning-dependent remainder (derived arithmetic, recalled GWPs, categorical/omission findings)The other 23 hard claims require a live model; recall/precision vary run-to-run and are reported per dated run, not as a fixed score

Honest scoring. The live scorer now performs one-to-one (bipartite) matching — each finding is credited to at most one ground-truth label and vice-versa, so a single broad finding cannot inflate recall, and unmatched findings are counted as false positives (never a negative "extra"). It reports two recall numbers: an executed-only recall and a primary recall that counts not-run (quota/outage) pairs as misses. One case (a 30°C supply-air setpoint, within ASHRAE A1 allowable but above recommended) is deliberately contested and scored against us, so the honest precision sits near ~0.9, not a suspicious 1.000.

Independence caveat. On one BMS pair, a live run surfaced two consequences we had not listed and we adopted them into ground truth; labels added after seeing the model's output are no longer independent of it, and we say so. A fully independent, frozen, primary-source benchmark with pre-registered labels is the next validation milestone.

7. Business impact

The asymmetry is real: an undetected deviation can become a multi-week commissioning slip, while an analysis costs cents. But the pricing, ROI multiple, payback, and margin figures are modeled hypotheses scenario — not validated by a paid pilot, LOI, or customer, and the ROI calculator is a sensitivity model, not realized savings. We have not yet run a timed manual-coordination study, so we make no measured hours-saved claim. The defensible business statement today: "The cost of late nonconformance can be high; Pramaan is designed to move detection to submittal review."

9. Security, standards & IP posture

  • No API keys are committed; credentials are external secrets. Path-traversal, malformed-input, output-escaping, and upload-size tests exist; error bodies never leak stack traces.
  • Third-party standards content is a team-authored paraphrase from public/secondary references — proprietary standard text is not redistributed; product/standard names are trademarks of their owners and Pramaan is not endorsed by them.
  • Known follow-ups (P1): auth + rate limits on public LLM endpoints, upload/dependency hardening, and a THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md with per-artifact source/version/hash.

10. Honest limitations & roadmap

  • Schedule risk is simulation, not field-calibrated prediction; deviation probabilities and "actual delays" are seeded, not empirical.
  • Supply-chain intelligence is illustrative — closed-form over static data, no live shipment/route feed yet.
  • No measured coordination-hours study yet; the 45-day figure was a UI constant and is not presented as a result.
  • Next milestones: a frozen independent benchmark (primary-source PDFs, hashes, pre-registered labels, clean negatives, one-to-one TP/FP/FN with variance); a timed human baseline; and auth + rate limiting. (Done since: OCR now ships in the hosted image via the Render Docker build.)

12. Anticipated judge questions (honest answers)

Is the data real? The 12-project portfolio is synthetic regression data. The evaluation pairs are team-authored with values cited from public product figures; 4 claims are checked deterministically offline, 23 require a live model. A fully independent, frozen benchmark is the next milestone.

Is schedule risk predictive? Today it is a seeded CPM / Monte-Carlo scenario engine, not calibrated on field actuals. That calibration is the next validation step.

Why "agents"? The LLM is used only for cross-document semantic reconciliation; validation, Cx mapping, schedule propagation, and guardrails are deterministic so outputs stay inspectable. It is one reasoning graph plus three deterministic services, not five agents.

What did users save? We make no measured field-savings claim yet; a timed human baseline is planned.
